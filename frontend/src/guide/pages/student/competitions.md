A competition is a timed, class-wide race through a few questions. Unlike a normal lab, **correctness counts here**: points come from passing the teacher's tests.

## Join

When a competition is open for you, a gold card appears on your home page. Press it to enter the **lobby**. Pick the name that appears on the board (up to 24 characters), or keep your roll number. Questions stay hidden until your teacher starts the clock.

## During the clock

All questions appear at once. Open one in the normal editor, **Run** as often as you like, and press **Submit** to run the tests. The clock is shown in the top bar.

- **Back to board** returns to the competition screen. Open the question again and your last code is waiting, in the language you were using.
- Some competitions allow **any language**; pick one per question with the Language switch.

## Scoring

For each question your **best** submission counts.

| | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Tests passed | 100 × passed ÷ total (partial credit) |
| Speed, only if every test passes and there is a clock | up to 50, depending on how much of the planned time was left |

A wrong submission costs nothing. Runs never score. Ties go to whoever reached the score first.

Badges recognise more than the top score: *First to solve*, *Fastest on a question*, *Solved them all*, *Most bugs fixed*. Nothing ever marks someone as low.

## When time runs out

You do not lose points for code you forgot to submit. When the clock ends (or your teacher presses **End now**), your editor stops, sends your last edits and returns to the competition screen. After a short grace period the server grades what you had written, as if submitted at the end. Those answers are labelled **At the bell**. Until grading is done the screen says final standings are coming.

## The leaderboard

What you see depends on your teacher's setting: the top ten plus your own place, everyone, or only your own score until the end. Other students' roll numbers are never shown, only the names they picked.

## Competitions and the detector

The leaderboard never uses anything from the review timeline. A "moment worth a conversation" can never cost you a place or points. Competition sessions are compared only with your own competition sessions, never with normal labs.
